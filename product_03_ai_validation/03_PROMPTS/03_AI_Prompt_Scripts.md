# AI Prompt Scripts for Demand Testing
## 5 Production-Ready Prompts That Extract Real Demand Signals

**Instructions:** Paste each prompt into ChatGPT or Claude. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your idea specifics. Save the outputs — they feed directly into your Scoring Guide and become your evidence base.

---

## PROMPT A1 — Reddit Demand Scrape
**Purpose:** Find real humans actively complaining about the problem
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Channel:** ChatGPT / Claude with web search

---

**Prompt:**

"I want to validate demand for [YOUR PRODUCT IDEA: e.g., a Notion template for freelance designers to manage client projects]. I need you to search and analyze real conversations online.

First, search Reddit for posts in the last 18 months where people are [DESCRIBE THE PROBLEM YOUR PRODUCT SOLVES: e.g., struggling to keep track of client feedback, missed deadlines, scope creep] in communities like r/freelance, r/design, r/Entrepreneur, r/smallbusiness.

For each post you find, note:
1. The exact complaint or question (quote directly)
2. The subreddit and date
3. The number of upvotes and comments
4. Whether anyone offered a solution or product in the thread

Then summarize:
- Top 5 most revealing posts (with links if possible)
- The most common frustration words/patterns
- Whether people seem to be actively seeking a solution (vs. just venting)
- Whether any products or tools were already recommended

Format the output as a bulleted report."

## PROMPT A2 — Google Autocomplete Validator
**Purpose:** Confirm people are actively searching for this problem
**Time:** 3 minutes
**Channel:** Google Search manually OR use ChatGPT to model autocomplete results

---

**Prompt:**

"I am validating demand for [YOUR PRODUCT IDEA: e.g., an AI-powered email inbox management system for busy executives].

Please model what autocomplete suggestions Google would generate for each of these search stems (use your knowledge of common search patterns):

1. [problem]: best [solution category] for [specific use case]
2. [problem]: how to [desired outcome] without [pain point]
3. [problem]: [audience] [tool category] reviews
4. [problem]: [specific problem] Reddit
5. [problem]: free vs paid [solution category]

For each stem, generate 5 realistic autocomplete suggestions that a real person would type.

Then answer:
- Do these suggest high intent or low intent searching?
- What does this tell us about the buying maturity of this market?
- What language would a landing page NEED to include based on these searches?

Be strict — only suggest completions that represent real search behavior."

## PROMPT A3 — Competitive Absence Check
**Purpose:** Confirm the market isn't oversaturated while also validating demand
**Time:** 5 minutes
**Channel:** ChatGPT / Claude

---

**Prompt:**

"I am building a digital product: [DESCRIBE YOUR EXACT PRODUCT, E.G., "A Zapier automation kit for real estate agents to automate lead follow-up"].

I need a rigorous 3-part market analysis:

**Part 1 — Search the obvious:** Search for "[your product type]" on Gumroad, Payhip, Etsy, Product Hunt, and Google. List the top 5 existing products and their approximate price points. Are they well-made? What's their weak point?

**Part 2 — Find the gaps:** Based on the products you found, what are 3 specific things they ALL fail to address for [YOUR TARGET AUDIENCE: e.g., real estate agents who are not tech-savvy]?

**Part 3 — Name your unfair advantage:** Given the existing landscape, what is the ONE angle, format, or feature that would make a buyer choose YOUR product over the alternatives? Be specific — "better than what's out there" is not an answer.

**Final verdict question:** Is the market underserved enough that a new entrant with a specific angle could gain traction? Answer yes/no with a one-sentence justification.

## PROMPT B1 — Problem Reframer (Deep Dive)
**Purpose:** Surface the emotional and identity-level dimension of the problem
**Time:** 10 minutes
**Channel:** ChatGPT / Claude

---

**Prompt:**

"My target audience is [DESCRIBE YOUR BUYER: e.g., mid-level marketing managers at small agencies who are overwhelmed by content requests].

I want to understand the FULL psychological dimension of their problem with [SPECIFIC PROBLEM: e.g., saying no to last-minute content requests from stakeholders].

Please produce a detailed psychological profile of this persona:

1. **Surface frustration** — What do they say out loud at work? Quote typical complaints.
2. **Hidden shame** — What do they feel guilty about but never say? ("I should be able to handle this")
3. **Identity tension** — What is the gap between who they believe they are and how they're showing up at work?
4. **False beliefs** — What do they currently believe about the problem that is actually wrong?
5. **Trigger moments** — What specific event or moment makes them most frustrated enough to seek a solution?
6. **Aspirational self** — If this problem was solved, who would they be instead? What does success look like?

Then write 3 different landing page headlines (under 12 words each) that would hit this emotional profile exactly.

Finally, name the 3 emotions your copy must trigger to convert this specific buyer."

## PROMPT B2 — Offer Stress Test
**Purpose:** Test whether your offer survives real buyer objections before you write the landing page
**Time:** 10 minutes
**Channel:** ChatGPT / Claude

---

**Prompt:**

"I am selling [DESCRIBE YOUR PRODUCT AND PRICE POINT, E.G., "A $37 AI workflow kit for YouTubers who want to automate their content research"].

I need you to stress-test this offer from the perspective of 5 different buyer types:

**Buyer Type 1: The Skeptic**
They have bought digital products before that were full of fluff. What are their top 3 objections to this product existing? What would make them say "this is another generic AI guide"?

**Buyer Type 2: The Overwhelmed One**
They are so busy they don't have time to watch a 3-hour course. What is the ONE thing that would make them bounce immediately? What time commitment is too much?

**Buyer Type 3: The Bargain Hunter**
They think $37 is too expensive for a digital product. What specific evidence would they need to see to feel the price is justified?

**Buyer Type 4: The Perfectionist**
They want to make sure this is the RIGHT product before they buy. What specific trust signals, social proof, or guarantees would tip them over?

**Buyer Type 5: The Fast Actor**
They saw this, felt the spark of interest, but are now distracted. What is the ONE thing that would make them come back and buy RIGHT NOW instead of saving it for later?

**For each buyer type:** Write the exact thought that goes through their head when they first see your $37 price tag. Then write the one sentence that would change their mind.

**Finally:** Based on all 5 perspectives, list the top 3 things your landing page MUST address to convert the widest range of buyer types."
