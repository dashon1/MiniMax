# Sales Script Prompts
## 8 AI Prompts for Writing the Entire Sales Conversation — No Friction, No Sleaze

**How to use:** Paste these prompts into ChatGPT or Claude with your specific offer details. Use them before outreach, before calls, and after calls. Each prompt generates content you can use immediately.

## PROMPT 1 — YOUR HERO STATEMENT
**Purpose:** Define your offer in one sentence that creates desire
**Use when:** Building your pitch, writing your bio, preparing for any conversation

```
Write a one-sentence description of my service offer.

Context:
- My service: [what you do]
- My ideal client: [who you help]
- The specific outcome: [what they get / the problem solved]
- The timeframe: [how fast]
- The emotional benefit: [how they feel after]

Rules:
- Write it as if the CLIENT is the hero, not me
- Use specific numbers and outcomes, not vague language
- Make it sound effortless, not complicated
- Maximum 2 sentences
- No jargon

Example of the tone I'm looking for:
"He helps consultants automate their client onboarding so they can take on 2 more clients per month without burning out."

```

## PROMPT 2 — THE SHORT PITCH (50 WORDS)
**Purpose:** A conversational intro you can use anywhere — DM, email, in-person
**Use when:** Writing your outreach message, introducing yourself in a call, your LinkedIn bio

```
Write a 50-word sales pitch for my service.

Context:
- I help: [ideal client type]
- Who are struggling with: [their current problem]
- By offering: [your service]
- So that: [the outcome they achieve]
- Specific proof: [a number or result if possible]

Rules:
- Conversational, like you're telling a friend
- No exclamation marks, no hype words
- Specific, not vague
- Lead with the problem, end with the transformation

Write it:
```

## PROMPT 3 — THE DISCOVERY MESSAGE
**Purpose:** Send before a sales call to make the conversation productive
**Use when:** Scheduling a call with a prospect, reducing no-shows

```
Write a short message to send to a prospect after they book a call with me.

Purpose:
- Make them feel prepared
- Reduce no-shows
- Get them thinking about the problem before we talk
- Set the expectation that this is a conversation, not a pitch

Context:
- My service: [what you offer]
- Call length: [how long]
- Who it's for: [ideal client type]
- What I'll ask them: [2–3 questions I'll ask on the call]
- What they can expect: [the call agenda]

Tone: Warm, direct, no pressure

Write it:
```

## PROMPT 4 — FOLLOW-UP AFTER THE CALL
**Purpose:** Send after every sales call to keep momentum going
**Use when:** Within 2 hours of ending a discovery call

```
Write a follow-up email to send after a sales call.

Context:
- What we talked about: [key points from the call]
- What they agreed they needed: [their main problem]
- What I offered: [your offer/scope]
- Next step: [they said they'd think about it / they want to proceed]
- Price: [if discussed]

Purpose:
- Summarize what we agreed
- Reiterate the transformation
- Make it easy to say yes
- If they said "let me think," address that specifically
- Include a specific next step (not just "let me know")

Tone: Warm, factual, forward-moving

Write it:
```

## PROMPT 5 — OBJECTION: "TOO EXPENSIVE"
**Purpose:** Respond when a prospect says your price is too high
**Use when:** During a call or in a follow-up email when price comes up

```
Write a response to use when a prospect says "[your price] is too expensive" or "I need to think about it."

Context:
- My offer: [what they get]
- My price: [your price]
- The value/result: [what this is worth to them]

Rules:
- Do NOT apologize for the price
- Do NOT offer a discount immediately
- DO reframe the value in terms of return on investment
- DO acknowledge their concern as valid
- DO give them a reason to believe in the outcome
- End with a clear question to move forward

Structure:
1. Acknowledge: "That's a fair concern..."
2. Reframe: "When you look at it as [outcome] vs [price], most clients find that..."
3. Proof: "In fact, [specific example or data point]"
4. Question: "What would make this feel like a clear yes for you?"

Write it:
```

## PROMPT 6 — OBJECTION: "I'LL THINK ABOUT IT"
**Purpose:** Follow up when a prospect goes silent after expressing interest
**Use when:** 3–5 days after a call where they said "let me think about it"

```
Write a follow-up sequence (3 messages) for a prospect who went silent after a sales call.

Situation:
- They expressed interest but said "let me think about it"
- It's been 3–5 days since our call
- They haven't replied to my follow-up

Message 1 (Day 3):
- Tone: Casual, no pressure
- One sentence about something relevant to them (not the product)
- A soft question about where they are

Message 2 (Day 7):
- Tone: Direct but warm
- Short — 3–4 sentences max
- Reference something from our call specifically
- Ask if the timing is just off

Message 3 (Day 14):
- Tone: Respectful, clear
- Confirm that you're moving on but keeping the door open
- Offer to connect in the future if timing changes

Context:
- My offer: [what you sell]
- Key outcome: [the main transformation]
- Key proof point: [one compelling data point or testimonial]

Write the sequence:
```

**Purpose:** First message to a stranger who could become a client
**Use when:** Reaching out to someone you've identified as a potential fit

