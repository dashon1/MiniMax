# B-Roll Insertion Guide
## Where and When to Cut to Visual Footage — The Rule Book

**The #1 mistake:** Creators talk for the full video. No cuts. No visuals. Just a talking head. Their engagement drops because there's nothing to look at.

**The fix:** Every 8–12 seconds of talking, cut to B-roll. Here's exactly where and how.

---

## B-ROLL PLACEMENT RULES

### Rule 1: The 8-Second Maximum

Rule: No single shot should run longer than 8–10 seconds before a cut.
Exception: When you're intentionally doing a slow pan or reveal.

Why: Modern attention spans. If the camera doesn't move or cut every 8 seconds, viewers start scrolling unconsciously.

### Rule 2: Cut on Action, Not Static

Rule: Cut when your subject is mid-motion — not when they're still.
Why: Movement masks the cut. The edit feels intentional and professional.
Exception: When the static moment is dramatic (e.g., a pause before a reveal).

### Rule 3: The B-Roll Must Be Relevant

Rule: Every cut must add information or reinforce what you're saying.
Why: Random B-roll breaks the viewer's cognitive flow. The brain has to re-establish context.
Exception: Mood-setting establishing shots at the start.

---

## WHERE TO PLACE B-ROLL

### Placement Point 1 — The Hook Transition (0:00–0:03)

**Rule:** Start with a strong visual. Do not open on a talking head.
**Formula:** [Visual hook] → [3 seconds in] → Your face starts talking

**Example:** "The fastest way to do X" — Open on a close-up of your hands doing the action. Cut to face at 3 seconds.

**Exception for TikTok:** Some of the highest-performing hooks are just face-to-camera. Test both.

---

### Placement Point 2 — After the Hook (0:03–0:05)

**Rule:** After the hook statement, cut to something that demonstrates what you just said.
**Formula:** [Hook claim] → [Cut to B-roll showing it]

**Example:** "Most people's biggest mistake is X" → Cut to a visual of the mistake being made, then back to face.

---

### Placement Point 3 — Every Key Point (Every 15–20 Seconds)

**Rule:** Every time you make a new point, show it.
**Formula:** [State the point] → [Cut to visual of the point]

**Example:** "The key is consistency" → Cut to a visual of a schedule, calendar, or daily routine.
**Example:** "The result was a 40% increase" → Cut to a chart/graph showing the growth.

---

### Placement Point 4 — During Numbers and Data

**Rule:** Numbers and statistics need visual reinforcement.
**Formula:** [Say the number] → [Cut to chart, graph, or visual representation]

**Example:** "90% of people fail" → Cut to a visual of 9 out of 10 failing.
**Example:** "$10,000 in revenue" → Cut to a visual representation.

---

### Placement Point 5 — Before a Transition (Every 30–45 Seconds)

**Rule:** Before you change topics, use a cut to mark the transition.
**Formula:** [Wrap up first point] → [Cut to establishing shot] → [Start next section]

**Example:** "Now let's talk about the execution" → Cut to a clean shot of the workspace or tools.

---

### Placement Point 6 — During Emotional Moments

**Rule:** When you say something emotionally resonant, let the face carry it — but back it with B-roll.
**Formula:** [Emotional statement] → [Hold face for 3 seconds] → [Cut to B-roll for the next thought]

Why: The face builds connection. The B-roll gives the viewer something to associate with the feeling.

---

### Placement Point 7 — Before the CTA (0:50–0:60)

**Rule:** Before the final call to action, cut to a visual that signals "this is wrapping up."
**Formula:** [Final point] → [Cut to clean shot] → [CTA on face]

**Example:** "That's the system" → Cut to a clean shot of the workspace → "Follow for more."

---

## B-ROLL TYPES AND WHEN TO USE THEM

### Type 1 — Demonstrations
**Use when:** You're explaining how to do something.
**What to show:** Hands doing the action. Screen recordings. Step-by-step visual.
**Duration:** 5–15 seconds per step.

### Type 2 — B-Roll Over Voice (Voice-Over)
**Use when:** The B-roll plays over narration (voice-over, not face camera).
**What to show:** Related visuals that reinforce what you're saying without needing to see you.
**Duration:** 10–20 seconds. Maximum 30 seconds before showing your face again.

### Type 3 — Cutaway Shots
**Use when:** You mention a specific object, place, or person.
**What to show:** The specific thing you're referencing. Build the cutaway into the script so you remember to film it.
**Duration:** 3–5 seconds.

### Type 4 — Timelapse / Montage
**Use when:** Showing a process that takes a long time.
**What to show:** Sped-up footage of the process. Coffee being poured. Work happening. Keyboard typing.
**Duration:** 5–10 seconds.

### Type 5 — Text on Screen
**Use when:** You're making a point that needs emphasis.
**What to show:** Large text on screen. Key word. Quote. Stat. Number.
**Duration:** 2–4 seconds. More than 4 seconds = viewer gets bored.

## B-ROLL SHOPPING LIST

**Things to film that you can use in almost every video:**

| B-Roll Type | What to Film | Where to Use |
|---|---|---|
| Workspace | Your desk, computer screen, notebook, pen | Business, productivity, education |
| Hands | Writing, typing, pointing, gesturing | Tutorial, how-to, process videos |
| Coffee/laptop | Coffee cup + open laptop | Lifestyle, productivity |
| Screen recording | Dashboard, spreadsheet, browser | Tech, business, analytics |
| Calendar/schedule | Open calendar, daily planner | Productivity, time management |
| Walking | Walking with purpose, city street | Relatable, vlog-style |
| Books | Stacked books, open book | Learning, education, personal growth |
| Phone | Phone in hand, texting | Social media, communication content |

**Filming tip:** Film everything in vertical (9:16). You can always crop to horizontal (16:9) for YouTube. But portrait footage can't be used in landscape.

## EDITING RHYTHM CHEAT SHEET

| Video Length | Minimum B-Roll Cuts | Recommended B-Roll Cuts |
|---|---|---|
| 15 seconds | 1–2 cuts | 2–3 cuts |
| 30 seconds | 2–3 cuts | 3–5 cuts |
| 60 seconds | 4–6 cuts | 6–10 cuts |
| 90 seconds | 6–8 cuts | 8–12 cuts |
| 5+ minutes | 1 cut per 15 sec | 1 cut per 10 sec |
